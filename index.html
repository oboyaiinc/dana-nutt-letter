I’m Dana Nutt.

I grew up in a one-room schoolhouse in northern Michigan. I swung a hammer for twenty years. In 1998 I bought ten acres for $7,500.

I am 69. Amy and I travel when we want, see the grandkids when we want, and hunt and fish when the mood strikes. Rental income covers the life we actually live. Not yachts. The right to wake up and choose the day.

That started with one small deal and a decision.

Pride doesn’t pay for retirement. Hard work, by itself, is not a financial plan.Dana Nutt

This is not a 401(k) lecture.

The standard advice (max the match, save ten percent, wait thirty years) is for people who started at twenty-two with a desk job.

If you’re forty or fifty and the account is empty, you don’t need a better savings plan. You need a different vehicle.

Mine was a piece of dirt in a small town the big investors ignore. Then another. Then a rental that paid me whether I showed up or not.

The letter is how I’d start if I were you this month.
